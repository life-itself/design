# Mood board

Images and references we like for the brand. Context is in the user's own words where given. See also [[design-system-plan]] and [[logo]].

This first set comes from the art manifesto, which was designed for near black-and-white print. The black-and-white images are the ones that best touch the essence.

## Patterns across the set (synthesis, 2026-10-01)

1. **Monochrome base, colour as an event.** Almost everything is black and white or sepia, with grain. When colour appears it is small, single and earned: the yellow field through the torn clouds, the daisy in the earth-caked hand, the red calligraphy stroke, the koi. This suggests the interface is ink and paper, and colour is a rare moment.
2. **Texture of the hand and the print.** Grain, halftone, ink, torn paper, rough printed edges. Nothing is smooth or digital.
3. **Hands and bodies.** The earth-caked hand, the braiding hands, the glowing fingerprints (which link directly to the [[logo]]), the dancers. The brand is embodied.
4. **Thresholds as doorways.** The torn opening in the clouds, the door-like frame with tiny figures, the pupil of the eye. Liminality is shown as an opening you pass through.
5. **Small people, vast things.** Tiny figures against a huge eye, frame, lily, mountain or root system. Humility and scale together.
6. **Science as beauty.** Root diagrams with depth scales, the 1899 fire-ball plate with its "Fig. 56" caption, the anatomical engraving. This is the clearest visual expression of **crafted rigour**, and the strongest antidote to woo-woo: wonder backed by observation.
7. **Hidden depth.** The roots spread far wider than the tree. A direct image of **deep sourcing**.
8. **Groups moving together.** People walking, dancers, a flock of birds: community as shared movement.
9. **Light from within.** The glowing fingerprints, the aura figure, the luminous birds. This is the most mystical strand and the one closest to the woo-woo edge; it needs care.
10. **Red is the colour of the hand.** Where a strong colour appears, it is often red and made by a gesture: the calligraphy, the koi, the logo's fingerprints.

### Design ideas that follow (to test, not decided)
- Ink-and-paper interface with grain; colour reserved for single moments.
- Scientific-plate devices as part of the system: figure numbers, captions, scale bars, fine rules. Rigour made visible, and quirky in the right way.
- Doorway and torn-opening compositions for heroes and thresholds.
- Public-domain engravings and scientific plates as a core image source. They fit the feel and are usually free to use.

### Things to be careful with
- The aura and glow images (two speckled figures, glowing hand) are the closest to the new-age register.
- Most of these images are inspiration only. Anything used on the site needs rights: our own photography, commissioned work, licensed images or public domain.

## Synthesis after the second batch (colour, more black and white, and "not us")

**It's about analogue process, not black and white as such.** In [[Sylvie on b and white]], the attraction to black and white is described as going to the essence: less distraction, older original photography, exposure, grain, texture, non-digital, craft. The colour images confirm this: their colour also comes from a physical process.

**Colours in the colour set are material colours:**
- Prussian blue from cyanotype (the hands, the butterfly)
- Deep red from etching ink and brush (Baobab Flower, the calligraphy, the doves)
- Gold and ochre from gold leaf and gilt stamping (the sun, the book cover, the sunflower)
- Muted teal, ochre and rust from cut paper on black (Grass of Parnassus, the swallows)
- Purple from bookcloth

**Usually one colour per piece.** Most colour images are one colour plus dark or paper: tone on tone, like a print run with a single extra ink. Colour is still an event, as in the first batch.

**Note:** red, deep blue and gold returned on their own, as materials rather than as a list. They match the emotional tones written earlier, which were set aside as a colour spec.

### Lessons from [[moodboard-not-us]]
- **Not wellness:** yoga retreats, sunlit arms raised, feel-good with no shadow (Plum Village, the yoga class, the Perspectiva essay grid).
- **Not luxury:** cinematic, exclusive, amber low light (Kempinski).
- **Not selling hard:** urgency banners, "top 1%", logo walls, star ratings (Dragonfruit, Aura Labs, the shop pages). Credibility must come from depth, not borrowed proof.
- **Not sleek and contemporary:** Perspectiva is closest in subject, which makes it the most important warning.
- **Tasteful isn't enough:** Sereth has cream and elegance but no personality. The near misses fail on character.
- **Not merchandised spirituality:** the enso turned into a logo on a t-shirt reads as a lack of care and rigour.

### Palette hypothesis (to test)
**Ink and paper, plus one ink at a time.** A warm black and an off-white with grain as the base. A small set of process colours (a deep ink red, a Prussian blue, a gold or ochre), used **one per composition**, never together, like a single extra ink in a print.
