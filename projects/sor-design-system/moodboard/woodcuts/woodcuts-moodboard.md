# Woodcuts mood board

Pinterest finds that show the kind of woodcut / block-print elements we want on the website. Focused on improving the woodcuts in `system/graphics/woodcuts/` (see [[../../system/graphics/woodcuts/README]]), separate from the general [[moodboard]]. Descriptions are in the user's own words where given.

Images live in `images/` next to this file, named `wc-NN-short-slug.ext`. **Not committed for now** (see `.gitignore` in this folder); we keep adding and decide later what to commit. This file is committed so the notes survive.

## What to take from the set

1. **Black plus one flat spot colour** (red), offset from the black so it reads as a separate printed layer (01).
2. **Form from line density, not outline** (02): a set of parallel swelling strokes does the shading.
3. **Warm speckled paper** under the ink (02), as against the cool off-white of 01.
4. **Solid black with cut-away white** (03) as the counterpart to line-built forms (02). Uneven inking and pitted ink texture are part of the charm.
5. **Silhouette as a floor** (04): pure black shapes with hairline stems, cropped off the frame, work as a lighter, more graphic option for backgrounds and borders.
6. **Fine engraving for the rigorous end** (05): stipple, dashes and hatching on white, for botanical-plate content. Too clean on its own; it would need print texture added to match the rest.
7. **A single warm colour works on its own** (06), with mustard in place of black: solid shape, white cut-outs, rough edges. A candidate for a brand accent colour; check against [[colour]].
8. **Muted complementary pair** (07): brick red and sage green, soft and slightly uneven, as an alternative to black plus one accent. A rough hand-cut border frames it like a plate, and the print-maker's mark sits in the corner.
9. **Grainy ink texture** (08): speckle in the solid black, from the paper showing through, is what makes a print feel hand-pulled. Worth adding to our woodcut SVGs (see also 03). Oak leaf and acorn is also a possible subject, for rootedness and slow growth.
10. **Rough, feathered edges** (09): ragged contours with a deep wine red, close to a crayon or loose-cut look. A warmer, richer red than in 01. Pairs with the single-colour approach in 06.
11. **A consistent family of motifs** (10): the same subject drawn several ways in one shared style. The stray gouge marks around the edge are a cheap way to suggest hand-carving in a vector.
12. **One spot of detail in a field of black** (11): keep most of the shape solid and put the fine cutting in a single place (here the acorn cup) to create a focal point. A sweeping arc composition with plenty of empty paper.
13. **Drawing with white gaps** (12): in a silhouette, thin white channels between shapes do the drawing instead of cut lines. A vertical strip works as a margin or divider.
14. **Dots on a line** (13): small solid round heads on a hairline stem, at varied sizes, with lots of space. A light and airy alternative to the heavy fruit and leaf blocks. Another small artist's mark in the corner, as in 07.
15. **Single-ink colours beyond black** (14, with 06 and 09): cobalt blue, mustard and oxblood each work alone on cream. Check against [[colour]] before adopting any. Note that 14 is AI-generated, so it is a colour/mood reference only.
16. **Cropped all-over silhouette in a soft colour** (15): branches running off every edge in muted ochre. Works as a background or section texture, and shows that the silhouette idea (04, 12, 13) is not limited to black. Possibly AI-assisted or digital in origin, so treat as mood only.
17. **Big, bold shapes in saturated ultramarine** (16): few large overlapping leaves, toothed edges, cropped off the frame. Cobalt/ultramarine blue now appears twice (14, 16), so it is worth taking seriously as an accent against [[colour]].
18. **Open, unclosed outline** (17): a few swelling brush lines, with shapes left partly open so the eye completes them. The lightest option in the set. A small red artist's mark in the corner (as in 07, 13).
19. **Paper grain as part of the image** (18): coarse mottled paper under a very light drawing does a lot of the work. We could add a paper texture layer behind the woodcuts (see also 02, 07, 08).
20. **Hairline fans against one dense form** (19): a pinecone as a dense, detailed cluster against long, thin needles. Needs a woodcut reading (solid scales with white cuts) to join the carved set.
21. **Bold scale pattern as black and white shapes** (20): a repeated organic form (scales, seeds, petals) drawn as chunky black outlines with white highlights, going ragged where the form opens. Strongest candidate for a pinecone element.
22. **One small metallic accent** (21): a gold-leaf sun on a black print. One small spot of warm colour gives the whole image its focus.
23. **A flock built from a few repeated birds** (22): the same engraved swallow rotated and overlapped into a rising diagonal. A cheap way to make a flock from one good drawing, and our core motif.

(More to come as the set grows.)

### 02. Apple, vertical-line engraving on speckled paper

![[images/wc-02-apple-vertical-line-engraving.webp]]

- Source: https://fr.pinterest.com/pin/242912973648274043/ (posted as "cheo.engrave", labelled Ho Chi Minh).
- User's note: an apple in woodcut.
- Noticing: single black ink on a warm, speckled, recycled-looking paper. ### 04. Hanging pomegranates, pure silhouette

### 05. Lemons on a branch, fine-line engraving

![[images/wc-05-lemons-fine-line-engraving.png]]

- Source: Pinterest (link not captured).
- User's note: lemons.
- Noticing: botanical-plate engraving style, in thin dark grey-black line on white. The opposite end from the chunky cuts in 01 and 03. ### 07. Tulips, red and green vintage colour woodcut

### 22. Flock of swallows, blue engraving

- Noticing: about eleven barn swallows in flight, in a single cobalt or ultramarine ink on pale grey.
