Seeds of Renaissance mark, variants

The current mark on every ground it has to work on, then the current swallow at a range of bigger sizes in the ring. Earlier tries (a rougher edge and three other birds) are kept below for reference.

1. Current mark

Separated into its two inks (red fingerprints, black swallow) so each version has a transparent background and can sit on any ground.

2. The current swallow, bigger

The original bird, unchanged in shape and direction, scaled up about its own centre. Fingerprints that would touch it are removed whole, so the ring opens up around it rather than being clipped. Choose a size to see it on every ground.

3. Bigger bird, keeping the fingerprints

In section 2, every print the bigger bird touches is deleted, so the inner rings thin out. These three methods keep the prints.

  1. Deleted. As in section 2, for comparison.
  2. Pushed out. Each print in the way slides outward along its own ray until it's clear. None are lost; a few pile up, as real stamping would.
  3. Bird in front. Every print stays where it was, and the bird sits on top with a thin white gap cut around it, flying through the prints.
  4. Pushed a little, then in front. Prints can move only a short way. Any still in the way are cut by the white gap. The prints come right up to the bird.

5. Small sizes: the simplification ladder

Starting from #4, each step makes the prints fewer, bolder and further apart, so the mark survives at small sizes. The idea is a family: the full mark large, a simpler step for avatars, the simplest for favicons. Use the tests to choose which step works at which size.

  1. #4. As chosen: rough bird +30%, all prints.
  2. A. Pale prints faded out. Only the strong reds stay, made more solid.
  3. B. Outer rings only. A, without the crowd of prints right around the bird.
  4. C. About 40 bold prints in two rings, still with fingerprint texture.
  5. D. 20 bigger prints in two rings. Smooth bird with a slightly thicker tail, so it doesn't break up.
  6. E. 12 solid dots in one ring. The bird bigger again.
  7. F. A solid red ring. The simplest possible: for favicons only.

Earlier try: the current swallow with a rougher edge

Same bird, same direction. Its perfectly smooth vector edge is what makes it feel commercial, so each treatment breaks that edge in a different way, the way a cut block or a print would. Fingerprints that would touch the bigger bird are removed whole, not clipped.

  1. Knife-cut. The outline redrawn as short straight facets, like a blade going through lino. The scallops on the wing stay.
  2. Ink bleed. The edge pushed slightly in and out, like ink spreading on paper from a block. The same quality as the fingerprints.
  3. Cut and printed. Both together: facets, then a lighter bleed.
  4. Carved line. Cut and printed, plus a broken white line carved just inside the edge, a common woodcut device.
  5. Grain. Cut and printed, plus a few thin white grain marks across the thick parts.
