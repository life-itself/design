Chapter 1

The feel

Every colour, face and layout decision is judged against one phrase, not against taste.

Grounded hope.

Hope stays the destination, and it is earned by going through the dark rather than around it. The sunrise painting is hope without ground; Dark Mountain is ground without much hope. We sit between: a skilled, caring presence at an ending and at a birth.

Supporting note

Deep sourcing

Going to the source, and the creativity that comes from there. Depth with the aim of really understanding.

Supporting note

Crafted rigour

Real thought, love and care in how things are made. The elegance of a potter's bowl, not of Vogue.

Supporting note

Liminal integration

Not opposites side by side but the moment they become one: dawn, birth, death. Crisis and renaissance as one transition.

Character

A little off-centre. Earthy, a bit muddy, a bit quirky, a bit marginal. Not mainstream, and not a wild artist's site either. The muddiness lives in texture and imperfection, never in muddy colour.

What we are not

Wellness or new ageLuxury, glossyCorporateHard sellingSleek and contemporaryChaotic artist siteLooking backwards

Three registers, one skeleton

Rigour is the form and warmth is the surface. Rigour is constant on every page (grid, type, accuracy, sourcing); warmth varies (people, texture, hand marks, colour, voice).

Chapter 2

Eight principles

The rules that carry the feel. When a new page is in doubt, check it against these.

  1. Ink and paper firstPages are mostly white, paper and ink. Colour arrives in photographs, not in the interface.
  2. Buttons are inkEvery button is black, solid or outlined. On night they turn chalk.
  3. Red is rareThe logo red appears only as an occasional link in running text, and footnote marks. Never a button, never a background.
  4. Pale yellow is light, not paintSunlight is a ground for a whole section. Never text, never a highlighter, never a bright yellow.
  5. One night per pageAt most one dark section, plus the footer. Dark and light always meet at a torn edge, never a straight line or a gradient.
  6. Polyamine speaks rarelyThe display face is for short statements: five to ten words. Pull quotes go to Restora, reading to Bricolage.
  7. Figures are platesImages are numbered and captioned like plates in a book: Fig. 2 then an italic caption.
  8. The hand is our texturePaper grain, torn edges, woodcut seeds and swallows, the fingerprints in the logo. Imperfection is made, not filtered on.

Colours have roles, not just values. Four grounds, three inks and one accent, used in that order of frequency.

Inks and the accent

Captions, labels, secondary text; hairlines.

An occasional link in running text. That is all.

Measured by area on the home page template. Photographs, which carry most of the colour, are left out.

Do
  • Let a photograph be the most colourful thing on the screen.
  • Alternate white, paper and sunlight sections to give rhythm without colour.
  • Use red for one link in a paragraph, at most.
Don't
  • Make a red button, a red heading or a red background.
  • Highlight words in yellow, or use a bright yellow anywhere.
  • Add a second dark section, a gradient, or a blue call to action (tried and rejected: too cool and corporate).

Chapter 5

Grounds and rhythm

A page is a stack of grounds. Rhythm comes from changing the ground, not from boxes, borders or colour.

Header · paper
Hero photograph
Statement · white
Pathways · paper
Selected work · night
Printed object · sun
Calendar · white
Interlude · sun
Engage · white
Footer · night

Sections run full width with the page gutter (16–56px) and vertical padding of 44–96px (.section). Each opens with a small muted label, then its content. Labels name what the section is ("Take part", "Coming up"); they are not numbered.

Alternate grounds: white for statements and lists, paper for collections, sunlight for joy and invitations. Never put two sunlight sections next to each other.

One night section sits roughly two thirds of the way down, where the page goes deep (selected work, a reading). The footer is the only other night.

Torn edges join night and light, and a photograph to the page below it. Give the tear the colour of the ground it tears out of: .tear--top inside a night section, .tear--bottom straight after one, .tear--overlay at the foot of a photograph.

Structure shows rigour: hairline rules between list rows, numbered figures, a calm grid of two, three or four columns that falls to one on a phone.

Chapter 7

Photography

Real people outdoors, doing things together, beside engraving, printmaking and honest art. Photographs carry the colour.

Choose
  • People and children in real places, warm and unposed.
  • Engravings, woodcuts, collage, drawing and printmaking.
  • Images that pair: something mythic beside something ordinary and human.
Avoid
  • Glowing sunrise paintings, auras, cosmic light: the new-age look.
  • Glossy lifestyle or luxury photography.
  • Too many black-and-white images together; the page goes cold.
PlaceholdersEvery photograph here and in the templates is from the mood board and not cleared. Joyful photographs with people and children are the top open need, along with rules on credits and rights.

Chapter 12

Open questions

What the system does not decide yet.

  1. Polyamine or Restora for headings? The mockups use Polyamine everywhere; the type notes reserve it for the hero. Test Restora at 24–34px among body text.
  2. Licences. Polyamine and Restora are not bought, so anyone without them installed sees Fraunces.
  3. Logo. Deeper red, redrawn swallow, small version, full set of versions.
  4. Photography. Joyful photographs with people and children; credits and rights.
  5. Woodcuts. Hand-drawn, or based on public-domain prints?
  6. Beyond the website. Slides, print and thumbnails need their own scale; the web scale here is fluid and will not transfer directly.
  7. Sub-brands. Do the magazine, podcast, courses and gardens get their own colour, or stay in one palette?
