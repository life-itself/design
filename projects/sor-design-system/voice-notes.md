# Voice notes: the media house line

A briefing for team discussion, 2026-10-08. The outcome lives in [voice.md](voice.md#the-media-house-line); this page keeps the reasoning behind it. Part of the [SoR design system](README.md).

## The question

What one line says what the media house does: the podcast intro, the YouTube banner and channel line, the About text? Rufus's direction: it is about *exploring*, and it should name **crisis** as well as **renewal**. It is not the tagline (*Vision and movement for civilisational renewal*), which speaks for the movement.

The test: **on-brand, but also powerful, accessible and resonant.**

## "Civilisational": heavy, but it does a job

The case against: five syllables, abstract, with a think-tank feel. On a banner it reads like a lecture title.

The case for: it sets the **scale**. Without it, *crisis and renewal* can read as personal: midlife crisis, spiritual renewal, wellness. That misreading is the real risk, because it puts us next to the self-help channels.

So the question is not whether to keep the word but **what else shows the scale**. Two ways to get the weight back without the adjective:

- **The noun, not the adjective.** *A civilisation in crisis* or *our civilisation* is concrete; *civilisational* is abstract. Same idea, much lighter.
- **Say "world".** *As one world ends and another begins* carries the full scale in words anyone understands.

## "A time between worlds": resonant for some, vague for most

- **For people in our scene** it resonates and signals the tribe. It is close to Zak Stein's *Education in a Time Between Worlds*, which ties it to the metamodern crowd ([voice.md](voice.md#words) steers away from that language on entry pages).
- **For everyone else** it is evocative but vague; it could be fantasy. It carries scale (whole world-orders ending) but doesn't say what is happening.
- **Where it belongs:** as the poetic second beat, after something plain has set the context. Never the only explanation.

*This* time between worlds, not *a*: *this* makes it now, and partly answers "which crisis?".

## What we already have

The best raw material is already written:

- **Hero line:** *For those who sense the old world is ending and want to help seed the new.* Plain, with the scale and the poetry, and no jargon. *Old world ending* does what *civilisational crisis* does, in words anyone understands.
- **Dedication** (the poetic version): *For all the living beings whose heart burns to see a new world emerge.*

## Options considered

The main options for the line, A to E (D leading):

| Option | Line | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Exploring civilisational crisis and renewal. | Names both; *civilisational* sets the scale and makes it one crisis, not many ([brand.md](brand.md)) | Heavy and abstract on a banner |
| B | Exploring civilisational renewal in a time of planetary crisis. | Renewal leads, crisis is the setting | *Planetary* reads as ecological, one symptom rather than the root; long |
| C | Exploring civilisational renewal in this time between worlds. | Poetic, no doom | Loses *crisis* |
| **D (leading)** | **Exploring crisis and renewal in this time between worlds.** | Both words plus the poetry; *between worlds* carries the civilisational scale without the heavy word; *exploring* says what we do; *this* makes it now | *Time between worlds* is insider language for newcomers (close to Zak Stein's *Education in a Time Between Worlds*) |
| E | Crisis and renewal, as one world ends and another begins. | Plainest way to say the scale | Longer than D and says the same thing less well; no verb |

Also considered for the banner, without a verb:

| Line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Making sense of the crisis. Seeding what comes next. | Strong two beats (sense-making and practice), but "what crisis?" and the civilisational weight is lost |
| A civilisation in crisis, and the seeds of what comes next. | The noun carries the weight lightly; *seeds* echoes the name; a little dark |
| As one world ends and another begins. | The most resonant, but says nothing about what we do |
| The old world is ending. What do we grow next? | A good hook; perhaps too casual |

## Where we landed (2026-10-08)

Leading: **Exploring crisis and renewal in this time between worlds.**

The first recommendation here was *Crisis and renewal, as one world ends and another begins*, to make the scale plain. Rufus's counter, which holds: *between worlds* already says that a world is ending and another beginning, in fewer words; the long version adds length, not clarity. And a banner needs a verb: *exploring* says what we do. Once *crisis and renewal* has set the plain context, *this time between worlds* lands as the poetic second beat, which is where this note says it belongs. The remaining risk (newcomers not knowing the phrase) is handled by the description, which spells it out.

## Build it as a ladder

Write the description first, then cut down. The heavy words go where there is room to breathe:

- **Description** carries *civilisation* and *a time between worlds*, once each, after the context is set.
- **Spoken intro** and **banner** stay plain.
