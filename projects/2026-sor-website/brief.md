# Seeds of Renaissance — Design Brief

*Draft 2026-09-28. This is about **who we are, what we offer and how we sound**. It deliberately says nothing about visual design: the look is open. Existing design work (the 2R site, the May 2R design system, the Canva deck) is **not** a guide and should not seed the visual search.*

## In one line

**For those who sense the old world is ending and want to help seed the new.**

## What Seeds of Renaissance is

A **media house and a set of spaces of belonging** for people who sense that an old paradigm is dying and something new is trying to be born. We offer the ideas, the practice and the company to help birth it.

The thesis is **one crisis, not many.** Climate, politics, meaning and technology are symptoms of something deeper: the civilisational story beneath modernity is no longer sufficient. Within that breakdown there is a real possibility of rebirth, a *second renaissance*.

We offer three things:

- **Vision**: a coherent, larger story that tells the truth about this moment and opens toward something worth moving toward. It draws on the best of human thought, art and tradition.
- **Practice**: embodied proof that another way is possible, lived out in farmhouses, studios, conversations and courses, not just argued in essays.
- **Community**: the company of others doing the same work, without pretending anyone has it figured out.

It is **a constellation, not a programme**. People share an orientation, and each finds their own expression of it. If you sense what we sense, you belong here.

## Identity: an alchemy

Seeds of Renaissance is one living world that holds together:

- **artist and philosopher**
- **feeling and thought, the sacred and the rigorous**
- **feminine and masculine**, embodied in its stewards Sylvie Shiwei Barbier and Rufus Pollock

This is not audience segmentation. Some parts lean one way:

- **Magazine**: poetic, sensorial, artistic.
- **Papers and essays**: philosophical, rigorous.
- **Podcast**: where the voices meet in motion.

These are expressions of one organism, and **the integration is the identity**. The site as a whole is the doorway where people find their natural entry point and gradually discover the wider whole.

## What we offer

**Media House** (discover, engage, make sense):

- **Podcast / YouTube**: one channel with stations. *Over the Mountains* is Rufus-led interviews. A Sylvie-led station is in development (working title *Temple of the Sacred World*). There may also be an explainer station.
- **Magazine**: a yearly art-led print periodical where artists and writers gather. Issue #1 *Impermanence*, Issue #2 *Mythos*.
- **Essays and white papers**: long-form, rigorous thinking about the moment and what comes next.
- **Art**: the art collective and its manifesto.
- **Greenhouse Newsletter**: releases, deeper thematic paths, events and opportunities.

**Gardens of Change** (belong, practise, create):

- **Courses and events**: practices people can take into their lives, plus in-person gatherings.
- **Community gardens**: online groups and meetups.
- **Local Gardens of Change**: people starting hubs where they live.

**The journey:** you sense something isn't right → you search for words → you find us (podcast, essays, magazine) → you find belonging → you practise → you seed something where you are.

## Who it's for

Entry point is **the sensing, not the conclusion**. We write for people who feel the gap before they can name it:

- **Meaning seekers** (mid-30s–50s): therapists, teachers, people in tech, health or consulting who read philosophy at lunch. They see the cracks and can't unsee them. Not in despair, not naive. Aesthetically sensitive.
- **Post-disillusionment renewers** (40s–60s): ex-church, ex-corporate, ex-academic, ex-activist. Through the dark night. Allergic to hype and urgency, and they trust people who admit they don't have it figured out.
- **Awakening professionals**: successful by conventional measures, they feel the system is off. They read widely but haven't found their people.
- **Disillusioned young seekers** (18–30): looking for meaning, belonging, a larger vision and real community.

What they want to feel: *"These people are honest. They're living it. I'm not alone in seeing what I see."*

**Not for** the cynical or given-up, who aren't seeking.

## Tone

- **Poet-philosopher and lucid thinker.** The lyrical leads and rigour grounds it.
- **Honest, rigorous hope.** Neither doom nor naive optimism. It is hope that has done the hard thinking.
- Speaks **from within** the crisis, not above it. Holds grief and possibility at once.
- **Kind authority, gentle curiosity.** Warm: people feel held, not lectured.
- Precise without being cold. Lyrical without being vague.
- **Avoid:** cold intellectualism, activist urgency, hype, insider jargon ("metacrisis", "metamodern", "interbeing"), wellness or spiritual-lifestyle gloss, corporate polish.

**Written pattern:** a lyrical opener that lands emotionally → a clear, lucid body → an implication or invitation.

## What the design must do

- Feel like a **living world**, not an organisation's brochure. It should be alive, not archival.
- Hold **art and rigour** together, so neither reduces the other to decoration.
- Let **different pages carry different centres of gravity** (magazine vs essay vs podcast) while clearly being one thing.
- Make the **first step easy** for someone who only has a feeling: listen, read, subscribe, come to something.
- Reward the second look.
