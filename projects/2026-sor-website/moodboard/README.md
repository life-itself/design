# Moodboard

Inspiration, not instructions. The visual search starts clean: no palette, type or layout carried over from existing 2R, Canva or earlier design work.

## Reference websites (from Rufus, 2026-09-28)

Reviewed 2026-09-28 in the browser. Notes on what each does and what we might take from it.

### Emergence Magazine: emergencemagazine.org

*Ecology, culture, spirituality. The closest peer in subject matter.*

- **What it does:** a slow, ceremonial entrance (the wordmark types itself in). Then the homepage runs as a sequence of large modules: a feature, the print volume ("Seeds and Structures", fittingly), a short **editorial statement**, selected stories labelled by form (POEM, ESSAY, FILM) and ways to engage.
- **Type:** one neutral grotesk (GT America) for almost everything, set in small caps-style labels. A single characterful display face for the wordmark (Windsor). Courier for meta.
- **Take:** each story gets its **own commissioned artwork and atmosphere** while the frame stays quiet and neutral. Content is **labelled by form** (poem / essay / film / audio). There is a **short editorial statement on the homepage** saying what they believe. Audio and meditations sit as first-class content. The print issue is presented as an object.
- **Watch:** the entrance animation is slow. It is beautiful once, then friction.

### Openhouse Magazine: openhouse-magazine.com

*Architecture, people, slow living. Print magazine with a web presence.*

- **What it does:** a full-bleed documentary photo hero with the issue title set in a large classical serif, partly italic ("Issue №25 / *Obsessions: A Self Portrait*"). Below, a **staggered two-column story list**: small images offset against each other, with lots of air, title and standfirst in a plain sans.
- **Type:** EB Garamond (classical serif, heavy use of italics) + Sneak (clean grotesk).
- **Take:** **warm documentary photography of real people in real places**, which is exactly our gap and our need (the farmhouse, the gatherings). An **asymmetric, breathing grid** that feels curated, not templated. Serif italics for poetry, sans for clarity.
- **Watch:** it is lifestyle-coded. Beautiful but "aspirational interiors". We need depth, not taste.

### ECC Performance Art: ecc-performanceart.eu/coursesandworkshops/courses

*Performance art courses. Very plain site.*

- **What it does:** a no-frills course listing: image left, then title, "with [tutor]", a two-line description, dates, fee, "View more". It explains remote/recorded access plainly. A **huge italic participant testimonial** closes the page.
- **Type:** PT Sans throughout.
- **Take:** **practical offers stated plainly**: who, what, when, how much, how to join. No selling. That fits "honest, we don't pretend" and the lucid register for courses and events. The **big testimonial as the emotional moment** of an otherwise plain page.
- **Watch:** it looks dated and amateur. Take the honesty and information design, not the finish.

### Cargo, Sites in Use: cargo.site/community/in-use

*Showcase of artist and designer sites built on Cargo.*

- **What it does:** a gallery of independent artist, typographer and architect sites. Tiny nav on a dark UI, sites shown in browser frames.
- **Common traits of the showcased sites:** **index-style layouts**, raw HTML energy, one strong or strange display typeface, monospace meta, image grids, publications shown as objects (book spreads, type specimens). One example (lucapetrucci-index) mixes **engraving and occult imagery with blackletter-ish display type**, close to our mythic register.
- **Take:** the **artist's-studio feeling**: made by people, not a brand agency. Freedom to be strange. Treating the magazine and manifesto as **art objects**.
- **Watch:** it can become cool and cold, and exclude people who aren't designers.

### Lee Holmes: leeholmes.uk

*Designer/developer for alternative clothing brands.*

- **What it does:** a **pixel/bitmap blackletter** display headline with a text-scramble effect. Scattered little pixel glyphs float around a white page. Then a very large grotesk statement paragraph. Project images appear **pixelated and resolve into focus** as they load. Monospace labels in parentheses ("(AVAILABLE FOR NEW PROJECTS)"). Asymmetric grid.
- **Type:** Neue Montreal (grotesk) + Xanh Mono + a custom pixel blackletter.
- **Take:** **old letterforms made contemporary**, a blackletter/manuscript echo reborn in pixels. That is a literal "renaissance" gesture: old forms, new medium. **Images resolving from coarse to clear**, which fits "seeds becoming". A **big plain-spoken statement paragraph** as the core of the homepage.
- **Watch:** the streetwear and gaming edge. It must be tempered for 40–60-year-old renewers.

### Across the five

- **Quiet frame, loud content.** The frame is neutral, and the artwork, photography or type carries the soul (Emergence, Openhouse).
- **One characterful display face + one plain workhorse** (all of them).
- **Label content by form** (poem, essay, film, course) and **state practical things plainly** (Emergence, ECC).
- **Print objects shown as objects** (Emergence, Openhouse, Cargo).
- **Old forms, new medium** (Lee Holmes, Cargo's engraving and blackletter sites).
- **Real people in real places** (Openhouse). This is what we most lack in current imagery.
