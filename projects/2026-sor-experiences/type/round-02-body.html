Seeds of Renaissance · type · round two

Body faces, and the small voice underneath

Two body faces in the real frame, then three for the eyebrow line. Only one thing changes per block. Through the body section the eyebrow is held at Apfel Grotezk; through the eyebrow section the body is held at Bricolage Grotesque, the face you chose.

The statement is set in Polyamine, standing in for Restora, which is commercial and cannot be embedded. It renders only on a machine with Polyamine installed — elsewhere that line falls back and should be ignored.

Where this landed

“If you take that, then you have to compensate with something more weighty and grounded in the body.”

Two survive, against Polyamine: Bricolage Grotesque first, Bespoke Serif second. Everything else is out and has been moved to the bottom of this page.

Worth noting what that result says, because it is not what I predicted. My pick was Bespoke Slab, on the grounds that it was the most weighty without being fat — and it lost, along with every other serif except one. A sans came first. So the contrast wanted between display and body is wider than a shift of weight inside the same family of shapes: it is a change of kind, not of degree. Slab weight was not the answer; a different skeleton was.

The eyebrow is still open, and is the section in the middle.

Part one

The body

Six candidates, ranked. All are free for commercial use. Commercial options exist — Arizona Mix from Dinamo and Right Serif from Pangram Pangram are the strongest — but they cannot be embedded, so they are not shown rather than not considered.

B1

Bricolage Grotesque

Mathieu Triay · free, SIL OFL

Chosen · first

The strongest contrast against the display face, and unmistakably drawn now.

A grotesque with ink traps and deliberately awkward details — the ink traps are the giveaway that it is contemporary rather than revived, which matters given how much has been rejected for looking backwards. Chunky enough to act as a real foil to a sleek display serif rather than a quieter echo of it.

B2

Bespoke Serif

Indian Type Foundry, via Fontshare · free for commercial use

Chosen · second

Sturdy and wide, with flared, slightly odd details.

The serif answer to the same brief. Low contrast, so no hairlines to flicker out on the dark frames, and the flared terminals keep it from reading as a neutral workhorse. Warmer and less of a departure than Bricolage.

Part two

The eyebrow

The smallest voice on the page: labels, captions, and the invitation line. It has never been chosen — the build falls back to the system font by accident.

One practical constraint that decided more than taste did: the line ends in a down arrow, and several otherwise good faces have no arrow glyph at all. Everything shown here carries both the arrow and the middot.

Apfel Grotezk

Collletttivo · free, SIL OFL

My pick

Flat terminals and slightly odd proportions — the quality you liked in Karla. Warm rather than cold-Swiss, sturdy at 12px reversed out, and it carries the down arrow and middot, which several candidates do not.

Azeret Mono

Displaay · free, SIL OFL

If you want texture

Mono gives the most tension against an elegant serif. It reads forward-looking rather than retro-terminal, which most monos fail at. The reservation: mono adds a fourth texture to the system and reads technical, which pulls against groundedness and wholeness.

Christian Thalmann · free, SIL OFL

The warm one

A humanist sans with Garamond bones — the most grounded and whole of the three, and the least edgy. Use 500 or above; the light weights disappear on black.
