More pages, with joy

Four more Seeds of Renaissance pages, one for each register of the site: a gathering page, a joining page, a magazine issue and a white paper. Text is placeholder.

What's new: light and joy

  • Sunlight. A warm yellow taken from the field in the mood board collage, not the Life Itself yellow. It is used as light: pale sunlit grounds, a hand-drawn sun, a highlighter on a few words, and one full yellow band on the joining page. Never for text.
  • Swallows in flight drawn across the pages, and a small sun mark: the poetry layer, taken from the logo and the mood board.
  • More people. Dancers leaping, women braiding hair, the shared meal. We still need photos with children and more everyday joy; none of the current images show children in a way we'd use.
  • A poetic voice: a soft italic serif for poems, quotes and interludes, alongside Polyamine.
  • The system holds: black buttons, red only for occasional links, white and paper grounds, the torn edge (now on the footer), numbered figures, contents and footnotes for rigour.

White paper · No. 3 · March 2026

The Second Renaissance: A Very Short Introduction

By The Seeds of Renaissance research group24 min readVersion 1.2
Download PDFCite this paper
Contents1Real risk2Root cause3Radical response4Real possibility5What we can do now·Notes and references
AbstractWe argue that the converging crises of our time share a root in how we make meaning, and that a renewal on the scale of the first Renaissance is both necessary and possible. We set out the case in four steps.

1Real risk

The risks we face are not separate emergencies but symptoms of a civilisation whose foundations are cracking.1 Ecological overshoot, political fracture and a loss of shared meaning reinforce one another, so solving them one by one will not be enough.

Every great turning carries within it the seed of rebirth. This paper is about that seed: what it is, where it grows and how it can be tended. For the longer argument, see the full report.

2Root cause

Beneath the crises lies a way of seeing that divides the world into parts and forgets the whole.2 Changing that way of seeing is slow work, and it is the work this movement exists to do.

  1. See Part 1 of the full report for the evidence on converging risks.
  2. On attention and the divided way of seeing, see McGilchrist (2021).
